# Atmosphere Governance

**Document role:** The design law for what may exist at Hideout — atmosphere vs clutter, inventory vs spaciousness.  
**Status:** Canonical.  
**Date:** June 2026

**Pair with:** `HIDEOUT_HOUSE_DOCTRINE.md` · `COMPATIBLE_STEWARD_FRAMEWORK.md`

You know clutter when you feel it. This document makes the intuition **operational** so successful activity does not become visual, operational, and cultural noise.

---

## The governing question

> **Does this increase atmosphere or increase inventory?**

Atmosphere may grow. Inventory must stay constrained. The goal is not minimalism — it is **spaciousness**.

---

## Definitions

**Atmosphere** — Conditions that change how the body feels in the room without demanding attention. Calm, coherence, warmth, aliveness, ritual, beauty that recedes. You notice the feeling; you do not notice the mechanism.

**Inventory** — Objects, signage, programs, messages, or decisions that compete for attention. Each item asks to be seen. Accumulation creates **noise** even when every item is good in isolation.

**Clutter** — Inventory that exceeds the house's capacity to remain calm. Clutter is not ugliness. It is **too many things asking at once**.

---

## Diagnostic tests

Run these before any addition — contributor, steward, or Brice.

| Test | Pass | Fail |
|------|------|------|
| **Attention** | Enhances the room without demanding notice | Requires notice to justify its existence |
| **Removal** | If removed, the room feels emptier but calmer is wrong — something real is lost | If removed, the room feels calmer — it was inventory |
| **Silence** | Works in quiet; does not need explanation | Needs a sign, speech, or story to land |
| **Nervous system** | Shoulders drop slightly | Eye scans, counts, or negotiates |
| **Coherence** | Feels like it was always possible here | Feels like it was imported or performed |
| **Subtraction** | Contributor suggests removing something | Contributor only suggests adding |

**Brice's instant test (from Matteo pilot):** *I love this, but it still has to feel calm.* Calm is not absence of activity. Calm is **no competing demands**.

---

## Atmosphere (may grow)

- Healthier garden, living plants, seasonal flowers
- Music that creates space — Sunday slot as infrastructure
- Meaningful gatherings with restraint
- Beauty that does not announce itself
- Ritual (open, service, close) without theater
- Expertise made visible quietly — craft, not performance
- Light, air, texture, smell — sensory coherence
- Compatible stewards adding **non-predictable competence**

---

## Inventory (must stay constrained)

- More objects without removal
- Signage explaining what should be obvious
- Decoration that competes with the terrace
- Programs that multiply without retiring old ones
- Founder merchandising, personal brand artifacts
- Visual noise — competing focal points on patio or corridor
- "More" as default response to success
- Enthusiasm without edit — the most common path to clutter

---

## Failure mode (Phase 3)

Hideout's future failure is not lack of activity.

It is **successful activity creating noise** — more contributors, more ideas, more visibility, more proof of life — until calm is gone and no one can name when it left.

Governance is not resistance to growth. It is **protection of the product** (conditions) while activity increases.

---

## Connection to steward framework

No steward promotion if pilot additions fail atmosphere tests.

Revocation trigger: repeated inventory adds after edit; territory demands; calm measurably degraded.

Brice's role: **protect atmosphere, coherence, calm** — even when that means saying no to good ideas.
