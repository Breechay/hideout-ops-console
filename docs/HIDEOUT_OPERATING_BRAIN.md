## Business Model — Four Modes, One Asset

Hideout is four business modes sharing one asset. Not "a café with side projects." See `HIDEOUT_HOUSE_DOCTRINE.md` for the house model.

**A. Transactional Hospitality**
Walk-in revenue, food/coffee, repeat customers. Daily infrastructure — consistent conditions, not the identity of the place. Without reliable hospitality the other three modes weaken, but hospitality serves the house; the house is not defined by the menu.

**B. Neighborhood Infrastructure**
Hideout as trusted neighborhood node. Watermarc residents, SkyView residents, concierge referrals, leasing relationships, dog walkers, park adjacency, local routine capture. Goal: become embedded in existing neighborhood flows, not generate generic awareness.

**C. Recurring Revenue Partnerships**
Hideout as supplier, not destination. Cold brew accounts, office coffee drops, resident amenity coffee, leasing event catering, fitness studio supply, run club coffee, corporate breakfast drops. One invoice can be economically superior to many chaotic walk-ins. Jimmy = $144.45/order.

**D. Operator Studio**
Hideout as productive operating environment. Planning, coding, product work, photography, videography, operations refinement. Operator Studio is leverage, not revenue. Its value is indirect unless explicitly monetized. Works because of a specific combination: same-building commute, 4AM wake rhythm, genuine affection for the space, high autonomous productivity. Most operators do not have this.

**Warning:** Do not use the romance of the space to excuse bad economics. If an hour produces no customers after sufficient conditioning — close it. Data decides.

## Governing Decision Framework — Nervous System Economics

Not all revenue is equal. $150 from 12 frantic walk-ins at 11:30AM is not the same as $150 from one recurring cold brew invoice. Same dollars. Completely different nervous-system cost.

**Governing question (locked):** Not "How do I make Hideout busier?" but "How do I make Hideout structurally calmer while economically stronger?"

| Dimension | Question |
|-----------|---------|
| Revenue quality | Is it predictable? Repeatable? High margin? |
| Nervous-system cost | Is it chaotic? Batch arrivals? Service pressure? |
| Identity coherence | Does this feel like Hideout, or does it distort? |

**Revenue quality matrix:**

| | High cash | Low cash |
|---|---|---|
| Low stress | Distribution (Jimmy model), neighborhood infrastructure referrals | Operator studio time, early morning stewardship |
| High stress | Peak Fri/Sat hospitality, large walk-in batches | Dead hospitality hours with no customers |

**Strategic sequence (locked):** Consistent conditions earn trust. Trust enables partnerships. Partnerships create clean recurring revenue. Walk-in hospitality is one condition layer — not the governing purpose.

## Menu Redesign — Locked Synthesis (5-Agent, May 2026)

Menu engineering problem, not a graphic design problem. The menu's job is to route behavior.

**What does NOT change:**
- Dark charcoal aesthetic — differentiates in GBP grids, signals intentionality
- 'Signature Fuel' section header
- 'A steady place to start.' tagline — does the brand job in five words
- Single-column layout
- No food photography — ingredient lists are stronger conversion copy

**Named items — locked treatment:**

| Decision | Locked answer |
|----------|--------------|
| Which items get signature treatment | Three only: Erik's Addiction, Brice's PB Bowl, Iced Breechay's Special. Scarcity preserves meaning. |
| Visual marker | Thin 1px warm white/cream vertical rule to the left of named item names only. |
| Named item name treatment | Slightly larger or heavier weight. Same typeface family. |
| Descriptor line | 2–5 words. Function or person. "Owner's daily bowl." NOT marketing copy. |
| Section placement | Items stay within existing sections. No separate top section. |

**Erik's Addiction modifier architecture — locked:**
Format: `espresso +2 · protein +2 · both +4` — directly under ingredient description, same indent, no line break. Consider changing "oat milk" to "oat milk base" — primes customization without stating it.

**Pairing logic:** Implicit only. Layout proximity. Move Cold Brew and Matcha Latte directly below the food section. No explicit pairing text anywhere on the menu.

**What not to do — unanimous across all 5 agents:**
No food photography · no combo pricing · no script or decorative fonts · no 'Chef's Pick' or 'House Favorite' badges · no separate Signatures section · no wholesale language in menu body · no loyalty program mention · no QR code on physical menu until there is a destination · do not rename Erik's Addiction or Brice's PB Bowl · no white/bright redesign · no calorie counts · no mission statement · no two-column layout

## Recurring Revenue Partnerships

**Jimmy Holmquist model:** Regular, former running partner. Runs monthly trail events. 3 gallons cold brew × $45 = $135 + tax = $144.45 per order. Invoice 10045, May 7, 2026.

**Pickup-First Doctrine — Hard Operational Principle:**
Recurring Revenue Partnerships are a supply business, not a delivery business. Pickup-first by default. Delivery only when structurally trivial. Delivery businesses quietly become time fragmentation, route management, unpredictability, and stress creep.

| Account type | Fulfillment model |
|-------------|------------------|
| Jimmy / event partners | Account picks up before their event. Zero logistics. Proven. |
| A Better You salon | Adjacent handoff — next door. Functionally pickup. |
| Expansive Biscayne | Pre-order pickup. Fixed window. Members pick up on their own walk. |
| SkyView 22 / same-building | Elevator logistics only. Pickup-equivalent. |
| Watermarc / across street | Short walk or account sends someone. Acceptable if value is high enough. |
| Route delivery (reject) | Brice drives or walks a multi-stop sequence. Violates doctrine. Reject unless economics are extraordinary. |

*Injury note (May 2026):* Pickup-first model means injury constraint does NOT block fulfillment — it only affects outbound relationship building.

**Two archetypes:**

| Archetype | Frequency | Revenue pattern | Stress | Examples |
|-----------|-----------|----------------|--------|---------|
| Event Partner | Monthly or episodic | Variable — event-dependent | Low | Jimmy (trail events), run clubs |
| Weekly Recurring Account | Weekly or bi-weekly | Stable — predictable invoice | Very low | Office coffee supply, building amenity, fitness studio |

Do not conflate. '10 Jimmys' does not equal $1,444/month — Jimmy is monthly and event-dependent. 10 weekly office accounts at $45/gallon/week each = real recurring infrastructure.

## Growth Bottlenecks — Priority Order

Model has shifted from "make the café busier" to "increase clean revenue density."

1. First clean recurring pickup account beyond Jimmy — one account changes the economics more than any other single move.
2. Adjacency capture — A Better You salon (zero-motion), SkyView/same-building (zero-motion), Expansive pickup (trivial-motion).
3. Threshold conversion — column boards + passive capture card.
4. Repeat regular retention — customers who already find Hideout love it.
5. Throughput ceiling — unknown until 30-day stress score data accumulates.
6. Broader digital discovery — hypothesis: NOT the primary bottleneck at this stage.
