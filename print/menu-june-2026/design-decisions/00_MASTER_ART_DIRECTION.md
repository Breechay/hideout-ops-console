# 00 — Hideout Miami · Master Art Direction
## Physical Collateral · June 2026

---

## What this is

Not a café promotion.
Not a loyalty program.
Not a launch campaign.

**This is infrastructure signage.**

The closest reference points are:

- Boutique hotel room-service card
- Museum membership envelope insert
- Private club notice on a bulletin board
- Gallery exhibition handout left by the door
- Airline lounge single-sheet fare

The feeling a person should have picking up any Hideout card:

> "Someone thoughtful put this here."

Not:

> "Someone is trying to get my attention."

---

## The single art direction principle

**Calm competence.**

Everything — type size, spacing, copy length, image choice — should be in service of one question:

*Does this feel like it was made by someone who doesn't need to convince you of anything?*

If the answer is no, remove the element causing doubt.

---

## What Hideout is NOT (visual)

These are the most common mistakes. Avoid all of them:

| Wrong direction | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| Coffee bean photography | Generic. Every café does this. |
| Latte art hero | Wrong product — Hideout's signature is the smoothie and the toast |
| Warm cream background | Immediately reads as "artisan café template" |
| Neon or bright accent colors | Wrong register entirely |
| Lifestyle photos of people laughing | Hideout is infrastructure, not aspiration |
| Star ratings or testimonials | Signals insecurity |
| "Best cold brew in Miami" | Wrong claim, wrong energy |
| Download badge bigger than QR | Backwards priority |
| Urgency language ("limited time") | Violates doctrine |

---

## What Hideout IS (visual)

The column boards on the wall are the reference. Study them.

| Right direction | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Dark void backgrounds | Creates focus, conveys calm authority |
| Cream type at rest | Not shouting. Just there. |
| Negative space as a design element | Confidence through restraint |
| One caramel accent per piece | Rationed = precious |
| Cormorant Garamond Italic for identity | Editorial, not signage |
| DM Sans for function | Clean, legible, unfussy |
| Prices in monospace | Data is data |
| Hairline rules to separate, not decorate | Structure as information |
| Real food photography (not styled to death) | Honest over polished |

## The design family

All six placements + the flyer are **one family**.

Same system. Different message.

A person who picks up the lobby stand and the Sunday exit card should immediately know they're from the same place — even if they never see the logo. The consistency IS the brand signal.

What changes across pieces:
- The headline
- The QR URL
- The supporting copy

What never changes:
- Background: dark
- Type: cream
- Accent: one caramel element
- Margins: consistent
- Grid: same underlying proportions
- QR treatment: always framed in caramel

## Reference worlds for mood board generation

When generating AI images or collecting inspiration, work within these four reference worlds. They are listed in order of relevance to Hideout.

### World 1 — Boutique hospitality collateral
Aman Hotels, Rosewood, Edition Hotels, NoMad.
Key feeling: The card is better made than it needs to be.
Visual cues: Dark paper, blind emboss, cream type, no color except one.

### World 2 — Museum and gallery print
MoMA member card, Gagosian exhibition handout, Dia:Beacon wayfinding.
Key feeling: The institution trusts its own intelligence.
Visual cues: Strong negative space, serif at rest, nothing decorative.

### World 3 — Private members club
Soho House membership print, Core Club amenity card.
Key feeling: Assumes you already belong.
Visual cues: No selling, just information. Dark, considered, unfussy.

### World 4 — Japanese specialty coffee print
%Arabica, Blue Bottle Japan, Fuglen packaging.
Key feeling: Precision in simplicity.
Visual cues: Heavy negative space, disciplined grid, ink on kraft or black.

## The one visual risk worth taking

The column boards already exist and are excellent. The print system should feel like a **pocket version of the boards** — same world, compressed.

The risk: **No images on most pieces.** 

The column board works because it has real food photography. The cards should work with **type alone** on most pieces, and reserve the one food image for the mini flyer only.

This is counterintuitive. Every designer's instinct is to add a photo. Resist it. A card that's pure dark + cream + caramel type will stand out because nothing else at a counter, lobby, or salon looks like that. It looks like it came from a different category of business entirely.

---

## Photography direction (when used)

Only used on the mini flyer and as a background on the lobby stand.

Rules:
- Real food, real light, no styling excess
- Dark ambient environment — terrace not studio
- No people unless unavoidable (and then: backs, hands, blur)
- Caramel and cream tones in the food itself reinforce the brand palette
- Film grain always — prevents the AI/stock photo look
- No HDR, no color pop, no filters that look like filters

---

## Typography as the personality

Cormorant Garamond Italic is doing specific work here.

It is **not** a display font in the conventional sense. It is a voice. When it says "Same building." it sounds different from if DM Sans said "Same building." The italic serif adds editorial authority — the kind you find in a private club notice, not a chain café menu.

Use it for:
- The primary message on each card (the one line that carries the piece)
- The wordmark "hideout" in header position

Never use it for:
- Prices
- Hours
- URLs
- Instructions

DM Mono carries all data — prices, hours, URLs. Monospace signals precision and honesty. Prices aren't styled. They're just accurate.
