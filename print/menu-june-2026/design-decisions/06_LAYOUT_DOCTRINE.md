# 06 — Layout Doctrine
## Editorial single-column · Navigation two-column

**Locked June 2026.** Not a style preference — a job assignment.

---

## The two questions (do not mix them)

### Question 1: Is the two-column menu board better than single-column?

**Yes.** Not close.

The middle panel of the column stack (`FIRST TIME` / **MENU** / `MADE WITH REAL THINGS`) proves it.

At 8–10 feet, a person instantly reads:

- Food | Drinks
- Price
- Where to look

No reading required. For a physical board at peak window, that's a win.

### Question 2: Is two-column everything better?

**No.**

The strongest pieces in the family are **not** menus:

- Same building.
- Two minutes away.
- Welcome to the neighborhood.
- Your usual — two taps.
- Order before you walk over.
- Same house. Different room.

These are **single-column editorial layouts**. They feel authored. They feel like a house speaking.

Restaurant-style Food | Drinks mockups (Siena-inspired, hospitality templates) look professional — but could belong to 500 places. Remove the logo and you lose Hideout.

## Job assignment

| Piece type | Layout | Why |
|------------|--------|-----|
| **Editorial / orientation** | Single column | Emotionally memorable. House voice. |
| **Navigation / operational** | Two column (menu board only) | Scan behavior at distance. |

### Editorial pieces (single column — do not change)

| Piece | Headline |
|-------|----------|
| SkyView lobby stand | Same building. |
| Salon card | Two minutes away. |
| Watermarc front | Welcome to the neighborhood. |
| Counter card | Your usual — two taps. |
| Watermarc back / counter alt | Order before you walk over. |
| Sunday exit | Same house. Different room. |
| Column top board | FIRST TIME? / START HERE |
| Column bottom board | MADE WITH REAL THINGS |

**Layout rules:** Left-aligned or centered. Generous negative space. One headline dominates. QR in lower third. No columns. No price grids.

### Navigation pieces (two column — menu middle board only)

| Piece | Structure |
|-------|-----------|
| Menu middle board | Food \| Drinks with vertical divider |
| Mini flyer front | Single column OK at pocket size — different physical interaction |

**Layout rules:** Category headers. Prices right-aligned. Divider does cognitive work. Less copy than restaurant menus — one line + price.

### Functional but still editorial (single column)

| Piece | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Office B2B card | Pricing list, not menu grid — single column |
| House card | Identity, not navigation |
| Mini flyer back | One action — single column |

## The column stack (why it works)

```
┌─────────────────────┐
│ FIRST TIME?         │  ← Editorial (single column)
│ START HERE          │
├─────────────────────┤
│ FOOD  │  DRINKS     │  ← Navigation (two column)
├─────────────────────┤
│ MADE WITH           │  ← Editorial (single column)
│ REAL THINGS         │
└─────────────────────┘
```

Top and bottom = house voice.  
Middle = operational scan tool.

If all three boards became two-column information systems, you'd gain efficiency and lose the house feeling.

## What the family view confirmed

When all pieces sit together, you stop seeing flyers and start seeing a **house**.

Recurring signals (keep all):

- Dark volcanic stone (`#14120F`)
- Cream typography (`#F5ECD7`)
- Restrained caramel (`#C8852A`) — one per piece
- Editorial headlines
- Negative space
- QR as infrastructure, not decoration

**Background:** Use `menu-background-family-grid.jpg` — unified texture that reads clean across the full family.

## Strongest pieces (build these first)

1. **Same building.** — Orientation in three words. Not marketing.
2. **Your usual — two taps.** — Repeat behavior without app-marketing voice.
3. **Welcome to the neighborhood.** — Generous, not transactional.

---

## Weakest direction (avoid)

- Restaurant-style hospitality menus as the design language for cards
- Food | Drinks layout on placement cards
- Boutique-hotel luxury drift without warmth
- Too polished — Hideout is inhabited (regulars, laptops, Sunday music, neighbors)

The system should leave room for humanity. Memorable because it feels **inhabited**, not because it looks expensive.

---

## Photoshop rule

Before placing type, ask:

> Is this piece **orienting** someone to a room, or **navigating** them through a menu?

- Orienting → single column, Cormorant headline, breathe
- Navigating → two column, divider, prices right-aligned (menu board only)

---

## Typography structure (L1 / L2 / L3)

Pieces are related aesthetically — make them related **structurally**:

| Level | Font | Job |
|-------|------|-----|
| L1 | Cormorant Italic | One observation. The whole card. |
| L2 | DM Sans Medium CAPS | Eyebrow, category, one supporting line |
| L3 | DM Sans / DM Mono | Body, hours, prices |

**Editing > styling.** Weakest pieces explain too much. Cut until each editorial card is one observation.
