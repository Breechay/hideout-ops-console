# 02 — Hideout Miami · Mini Menu Flyer
## The one asset that does five jobs

---

## Why this piece matters most

The mini flyer is the highest-leverage print piece in the system.

Unlike the placement cards (which each serve one location), the flyer is:
- Attached to every to-go order bag
- Left at Watermarc concierge
- Handed at salon checkout
- Distributed at Sunday Morning exit
- Dropped at office manager desks

**One designed asset. Five distribution channels.**

Design this first. Get it right. Then the placement cards are variations on the same system.

---

## Format decision

**4" × 9" (one-third of a letter sheet)**

Why this size:
- Fits in a to-go bag naturally
- Long enough to carry the full menu
- Narrow enough to not feel like a flyer
- Folds once to business-card width if needed
- Single 8.5×11" sheet prints three at once (efficient)

**Alternative: 5.5" × 8.5" (half-sheet)**
If the 4×9" feels cramped with the full menu, go half-sheet.
More breathing room. Slightly more presence when handed to someone.

**Print quantity:** 200–500 for first run. Reprint when below 50.

### Typography decisions (front)

- Logo: Cormorant Garamond Regular (not italic at small size) + icon
- Category labels: DM Sans Medium, 8pt, uppercase, +80 tracking
- Item names: DM Sans Regular, 10–11pt, cream
- Prices: DM Mono, 10–11pt, cream, right-aligned to a tab stop
- Hours: DM Mono, 9pt, muted cream 70%
- Hairline rules: Cream 8% opacity — structure, not decoration

### Note on the menu content
The food inspector–approved PDF is the canonical source.
Use those exact item names and prices. Do not paraphrase.
Consumer advisory is required by law — include in fine print at bottom if this is posted publicly. On to-go inserts, check local requirements.

## Back: The Action Side

**Job:** One action. That's it.

The back does not repeat the menu. It does not explain Hideout. The front did that.
The back converts the person holding the card into a repeat customer.

### Layout wireframe

### Typography decisions (back)

- Primary message: Cormorant Garamond Italic, 38pt — this is the hero
- The caramel accent: a single short rule below the headline (not a full-width rule — it should feel like a breath, not a divider)
- QR: standard framing per brand system — caramel border
- Everything below QR: DM Sans Regular, smaller, quieter

### QR URL for this piece
`https://hideoutmiami.com/app?source=nfc`

The mini flyer uses the NFC/counter source because it's multi-context — you can't know where it was handed, but most will be at point of transaction or near the counter.

---

## Key design decisions for this piece

1. **No photograph on the front.** The menu IS the content. A photo would compete. Let the type and the items speak.

2. **No photograph on the back.** The back is one action. Any image competes with the QR.

3. **The caramel accent on the back is the horizontal rule, not the QR frame.** The rule draws the eye from the headline down to the QR. It's directional. The QR itself is functional, not decorative, so give it a clean cream-on-dark treatment with a simple caramel border.

4. **Prices right-aligned.** Set a right-side tab stop for all prices. The eye should be able to scan vertically down the right column to see the price list.

5. **No descriptions on the mini flyer.** The full menu board has descriptions. The flyer is the pocket version — name and price only. If someone wants to know what Shroom Coffee is, they ask.

6. **Sunday is not on this flyer.** Sunday is a room, not a product. If someone asks about Sunday on a weekday, you tell them. The flyer stays focused on the transaction.

Once the base template is done, prepare these variants:

| Variant | Change | Use case |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Back QR = ?source=nfc | Counter, to-go bags |
| Watermarc | Back QR = ?source=watermarc | Concierge packets |
| Salon | Back QR = ?source=neighbor | Salon distribution |
| Sunday takeaway | Back QR = ?source=weekday, add "Every Sunday 11–2" below rule | Sunday exit bridge |

The only change between variants is the QR code and 1–2 lines of copy. Everything else is identical. This is why getting the base right matters.
